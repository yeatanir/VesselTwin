# Track M results: reactive substrate sink and wall-uptake bracketing (pre-registered in docs/prereg/track-m.md, commit 02e103f)

Scored once (`python -m bench.track_m_score --score --write` -> `bench/track_m/results.json`); M3 file `bench/track_m/flux_vs_distance.json`. **All items are development data. The closure is a proposed reduced-order closure, not a validated MoS2 kinetic law, proposed after seeing the Track K/L failures; all its constants are synthetic (no source opened).** Even an improvement would not be a validation; there is none. n is tiny; one or two items are not evidence. Default model behaviour is unchanged (bit-for-bit test against the stored reference).

## Verdict
**The reactive sink does not rescue the benchmark, and it does not fix the boat-temperature insensitivity far from the boat.** Strict accuracy of the structural ensemble equals the single-model rule on every headline table under the paper-geometry rule (1/9, 1/8, 0/3, 1/12) and is lower under the default-geometry rule (1/9 vs 3/9, 1/8 vs 2/8, 3/12 -> 1/12), with abstention (unresolved) 0.78-0.89 on the s items. The hypothesis is therefore only partly supported: a reactive sink does remove the exact-zero-flux artefact (a substrate as hot as the boat, or hotter than saturation), but the response of the flux to boat temperature at the default 12 cm is controlled by the wall closure, not by the substrate closure (M3). That rules out "the substrate condensation closure is the cause of the boat-T insensitivity" as stated, and leaves the wall-uptake closure (NTU ~ 11, Track K) as the controlling assumption, which is exactly the part the stored CFD cannot independently test.

Switches `substrate_uptake_model` {condensation, reactive} and `wall_uptake_model` {condensation, reflecting, sticking, reactive}; equations, assumptions and plausible ranges in `vessels/cvd_tube/reactive.py` and `docs/prereg/track-m.md` section 1-2. ## M3: flux versus distance (default conditions, nominal and p05-p95 over 100 draws; mol MoO3 units/m2/s)
Condensation substrate, condensation wall (current model): exactly 0 for 0.5-10 cm (substrate hotter than the saturation of the arriving gas), 1.2e-6 at 12, 9.5e-8 at 15, 4.1e-9 at 20 cm; 71 % of MC draws give zero flux. Reactive substrate with the default (illustrative) constants: 2.66e-6 at 0.5-4 cm (the boat supply), 2.40e-6 at 10, 8.7e-7 at 12 (condensation wall) or 2.0e-6 (reflecting wall), 2.0e-8 / 4.8e-7 at 15, 2.9e-14 / 1.3e-11 at 20 cm. Reactive wall: 1.4e-6 at 0.5 cm falling to 1.5e-10 at 12 cm. The p05-p95 band of the reactive cells spans about 5 orders of magnitude at <= 8 cm (2.7e-11 to 5.3e-6): the kinetics are unconstrained, only the ceiling (transport limit) is known. With the default constants k_rxn is about 0.012 m/s at 861 K, comparable to k_m, so the default is not a limiting case; the ensemble spans both limits.

**Robust across all 12 variants:** (1) the flux falls by orders of magnitude between about 10 and 20 cm; (2) at 12 cm the boat-temperature response over 800 -> 850 C (liquid law, Track K's setting) is d ln F = 0.00-0.08 for every variant that has a sticking-capable wall (condensation; sticking >= 1e-2; both substrate models), i.e. the saturation seen in Tracks K/L persists with the reactive substrate; (3) the supply plateau at 0.5-4 cm exists only for the reactive substrate (the condensation substrate there has zero or near-supply-independent flux).
**Variant-dependent:** (a) boat-T response at 12 cm: restored (0.86, equal to the supply change) only by a reflecting wall, for either substrate model; 0.08 at sticking 1e-4; so it is the wall variant, not the substrate closure, that decides it; (b) the sign of the flow response at 12 cm (50 -> 200 sccm): +0.46 (condensation/condensation), -1.17 (condensation substrate, reflecting wall), -1.59 (reactive, reflecting wall), -0.01 (reactive, condensation wall), +15.5 (reactive wall); (c) whether the flux is zero or non-zero at 0.5-10 cm (substrate model); (d) absolute magnitude (reactive wall is 1-4 orders lower at 2-12 cm). Values of order 1e2-1e3 in `responses_nominal_by_distance_cm` for condensation/reactive at 1 cm are floor artefacts (flux of order zero versus non-zero), not responses.

## What this does and does not establish
- It does establish: (i) the exact-zero flux for a hot substrate is a property of the condensation closure and disappears with a reactive sink; (ii) the boat-temperature saturation at the default distance is not caused by the substrate closure: it persists in the reactive model unless the wall is reflecting (or almost, sticking <= 1e-4); (iii) the sign of several benchmark responses (s01, s03, s04, s07, s08, s10, s13) depends on structural choices that no source constrains, which the ensemble reports as unresolved rather than a single wrong call.
- It does not establish any kinetics, any improvement in accuracy, or that the wall is reflecting. The wall-uptake result is an assumption-sensitivity result: stored CFD uses a similar wall law, so it is not independent evidence for either wall closure.

## Not done / caveats
Sulfur-dependent reactive wall uses the same constants as the substrate (cheap variant). The gap model (s08, s13) has no wall, so the four wall variants are duplicates there (equal weights over-count the reactive substrate share). Equal cell weights are arbitrary. Members mix i in {1,2,3} and E_eff corners. The substrate-offset sigma (30 K, mean 0) is symmetric because no direction is sourced. The shape item s11 uses 64 members and no substrate-offset sigma. The post-hoc law and offset runs are exploratory.
