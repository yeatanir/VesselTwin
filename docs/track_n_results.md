# Track N results: furnace, quartz, gas-flow and boat/substrate temperature model (pre-registered in docs/prereg/track-n.md; commits 851210c and d2d6c16, both before the scoring run)

Scored once (`python -m bench.track_n_score --score --write` -> `bench/track_n/results.json`). Opt-in switch `thermal_model = furnace` (default `legacy`; the default results are bit-for-bit unchanged, tested against `bench/track_n/default_reference.json`, captured before the first model edit). **Verdict in one paragraph:** the gas-side description (gas heating, centreline versus bulk temperature, expansion velocity, residence time) agrees with the stored OpenFOAM tube cases to 25 K and 15 % in 45 of 52 cases (49 of 52 when the model is given the CFD's own constant gas properties) against 35 of 52 for the legacy model, but that test is a consistency check against a CFD that imposes the wall temperature, has no gravity and no radiation, and it cannot test the quartz, boat or substrate parts. The one published data set that could be compared (Momeni 2018, a simulation, not a measurement) is **not** reproduced: the model's gas peak is 143 K above the figure's 1050 K, because the digitised wall profile itself peaks at 1194 K. No measured in-tube gas or substrate temperature could be found and opened, so the absolute boat and substrate temperatures are **not validated** and rest on synthetic constants.

## What was built (`vessels/cvd_tube/furnace.py`, switches in `__init__.py`)
- **N1** parametric profile (1-3 zones of equal length over `L_hot_cm`, erf end ramps with `ramp_cm` = 10-90 % distance, erf zone blends, offset) or a measured table `x_cm:T_C;...` (extra key `wall_profile_table`, deliberately not a ParamDef because the dashboard builds a numeric input for every non-choice ParamDef); boats relative to the tube centre (`positions_from_centre`).
- **N2** quartz: element -> gap radiation (effective emissivity of parallel plates) + gap conduction -> wall conduction -> inner surface; two-band quartz (transparent below `quartz_cutoff_um`, opaque above; Planck band fractions) so the element heats boat and substrate directly; axial conduction along the quartz not solved, fin length reported (0.49 cm vs 12 cm ramp: negligible).
- **N3** gas: `energy_2d` (axisymmetric energy equation, prescribed Poiseuille mass-flux profile, axial + radial conduction, T-dependent k, hybrid scheme: gives centreline and mixing-cup T) or `graetz_1d` (Hausen developing-flow Nusselt march). u(x) from the ideal-gas expansion (factor T/273.15 exact, tested), Re, Pe (thermal Re Pr and mass u d/D_MoO3), entrance lengths, Gr, Ri = Gr/Re^2 with flag, residence time boat -> substrate (plug and centreline bounds), Taylor-Aris axial dispersion, `carried_by`.
- **N4** boat and substrate: steady radiative (two band) + convective + contact balance with local quartz, gas, element; lumped time constant and the lag behind a ramp of `furnace_ramp_K_min`; `boat_temperature = input` keeps the legacy inputs.
- **N5** `furnace.profile(values, n=200, n_mc=30)`; schema in the module docstring; p05/p95 from the ParamDef sigmas of `furnace.FDEFS` plus T_furnace_C, flow_sccm, Nu, D_scale, T_ends_C.

## Test (a): 52 stored OpenFOAM tube cases (12 `v5agree`, 40 `v5sweep`; all settled, all extracted; none excluded)
Stations: MoO3 boat strip centre and substrate centre (scored), release station (reported). Thresholds: 25 K (bulk and centreline), 15 % (velocity), pre-registered. The CFD wall (piecewise constant, including boat and substrate strips) is the model's wall; `quartz_coupling` is not involved. **The CFD has gravity (0 0 0): it cannot say whether buoyancy matters.**

| model | M1 bulk T pass (n=52) | M2 centreline pass | M3 velocity pass | M4 Ri regime pass | overall (M1+M2+M3, both stations) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| furnace `energy_2d`, NIST Ar (headline) | 48 | 46 | 52 | 37 | **45** |
| `energy_2d`, CFD constant properties (diagnostic) | 50 | 51 | 52 | 33 | 49 |
| furnace `graetz_1d` (Hausen) | 34 | 35 | 52 | 48 | 29 |
| legacy `march` (its mean also used as centreline) | 36 | 49 | 51 | 26 | 35 |

Median |error| at the substrate (boat in brackets), p90 at the substrate:
| model | bulk T, K | centreline T, K | velocity, rel |
|---|---|---|---|
| `energy_2d` NIST | 2.0 (2.1), p90 19 | 3.1 (2.0), p90 27 | 0.0013, p90 0.032 |
| `energy_2d` const | 0.8 (2.1), p90 2.4 | 1.6 (2.0), p90 4.1 | 0.0010, p90 0.004 |
| `graetz_1d` | 8.2 (7.5), p90 40 | 2.7 (18.8), p90 20 | 0.0084, p90 0.049 |
| legacy | 9.3 (10.2), p90 49 | 3.2 (5.9), p90 14 | 0.0095, p90 0.059 |

What this shows, and does not:
- With the CFD's own constant properties the 2-D model reproduces the CFD (substrate median 0.8 K; 3 failures remaining, all at the boat strip, 25.5-28.5 K). So the energy solver and the velocity bookkeeping are consistent with OpenFOAM. With NIST argon properties the 7 failures (v5sweep_02, 06, 09, 11, 23, 34, 37; 25-61 K) are mostly **property** differences: the CFD freezes k at T_furnace while the model uses k(T). Which one is closer to a real tube is not tested here (NIST is the better description of argon; the CFD is not a measurement).
- Legacy is better than `energy_2d` (NIST) on the **bulk** temperature in 4 of 52 cases, on the **centreline** in 22 of 52 (legacy centreline = its mean, and the CFD axis-minus-bulk gap is within a few K at most stations; legacy has the smaller centreline p90, 14 K vs 27 K), and on velocity in 9 of 52. v5sweep_11 and v5sweep_37 pass for legacy and fail for the model. The Hausen `graetz_1d` march is no better than legacy on the bulk temperature: it has no axial conduction, and Pe_thermal is 1-3 at 100 sccm, so the Graetz assumption (Pe >~ 100) is violated in this tube; that is the reason `energy_2d` is the default.
- M4: the flag Ri > 1 is true in 50 of 52 CFD cases and in 50 of 52 model cases, so it has almost no discriminating power here; the pass counts of M4 are driven by the factor-2 ratio of the span maximum, which in the CFD is sensitive to the 2.5 mm boat strip, and should not be read as a regime validation.
- The 52 cases are one code and one physics; they are not independent evidence. The pass fractions are descriptive.

## Test (b): Momeni et al. 2018 (SI Fig. S2 wall profile -> SI Fig. S7 simulated gas profile)
Inputs as pre-registered (digitised S2, +-5 C reading, ends +-20/15 C; 40 mm tube, 100 sccm Ar, 710 Torr; falling-edge alignment shift 59.0 cm; pads to 25 C beyond the digitised range). The article and SI are open access (CC BY); the 18 digitised wall points and the digitised S7 curve are stored in `bench/track_n/momeni_digitized.json` as derived data with the reading uncertainties.

| model | peak gas T, K | peak minus S7 (1050 K), K | RMS over heated region, K | pass (50 K) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `energy_2d` bulk (coupling off / on) | 1193.0 / 1193.0 | +143 / +143 | 123 / 123 | fail |
| `energy_2d` centreline | 1192.7 | +143 | 122 | fail |
| `graetz_1d` | 1193.8 | +144 | 126 | fail |
| legacy Graetz lag, same wall | 1193.8 | +144 | 126 | fail |

**FAIL, as predicted.** At Re = 1.5 in a 40 mm tube every model closes the gas to the wall within centimetres, so the gas peak equals the wall peak (1194 K): the difference is between the digitised wall profile (max 921 C) and the S7 gas curve (1050 K), not between gas models. Candidate explanations, none tested: the S2 wall profile belongs to a different run than the 800 C growth of the text; the COMSOL model's wall condition is not what S2 shows (its gas floor at both ends is 655 K while the S2 wall falls to 65 C at the right end); the simulation is 2-D with its own geometry. The 4 K drop near the crucible is not testable (no crucible geometry). Nothing was changed to improve this.

## Not scored: sensitivity (no data) and diagnostics
- Quartz and boat sensitivity (`results.json: sensitivity`): at the default plateau the boat and substrate sit within 1 K of the local quartz temperature for every setting of quartz emissivity (0.5-0.95), cutoff (3-5 um), transmission, boat absorptivity and body Nu, because radiation and contact dominate the gas and the element, quartz and gas coincide there. Steady-state boat-minus-wall differences are therefore small by construction of the physics; what moves is the **transient**: time constants 52 s (boat, 5 g, synthetic) and 5 s (substrate, 0.12 g) give a lag of 17 K and 1.7 K behind a 20 K/min ramp (boat lag 12-32 K over the boat absorptivity range). Quartz inner wall minus element: up to 7 K at the steepest ramp (heat belt edge) at 100 sccm.
- Benchmark-relevant diagnostic (`results.json: diagnostic`, as pre-registered, 9 configurations, furnace/legacy ratios): with the furnace erf profile at the legacy `ramp_cm` and the boat temperature kept as input, the substrate temperature changes by up to -6.5 % (862 -> 810 K at the default), the MoO3 supersaturation by x0.29-x16, the substrate flux by x0.11-x5.8 and the boat release (supply) not at all (x1.0, same boat temperature). With the computed boat temperature (the boat takes the local wall temperature, 749 C instead of the 700 C input at the default) the supply changes by x0.96-x111, the flux x0.12-x76, the supersaturation x0.86-x135. **Post-hoc, not pre-registered** (`bench/track_n/posthoc.json`): most of the profile effect is the ramp definition (legacy tanh scale ramp/3 has a 10-90 % distance of 0.73 ramp; the furnace erf takes `ramp_cm` as the 10-90 % distance). With the ramp matched (7 configurations), boat input: substrate temperature -0.9 to +0.5 %, supersaturation x0.79-x1.65, flux x0.92-x2.26, supply x1.0; boat computed: supply x1.0-x64, flux x1.06-x255, supersaturation x1.5-x27. **The benchmark-relevant quantities are sensitive to how the wall profile and the boat temperature are defined, and this track does not reduce that uncertainty with data.** A diagnostic, not a score.

## Sourced, synthetic, not validated
- **Sourced (opened):** NIST argon cp, mu, k at 0.101325 MPa, 300-1400 K (https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/fluid.cgi?ID=C7440371; EOS range noted 'to 700 K' for density/cp, extrapolation reasonable; k 2 %, mu 0.5-2 %); fused-quartz conductivity 1.9 W/m/K read from the GE 124 datasheet figure (2spi.com PDF; printed 1.4 W/m/K at room T; the 500-1000 C part is marked 'ESTIMATED'; +-0.15), quartz transmission 0.9 and the 4 um cutoff read from its transmittance figure; Hausen Nusselt form and Nu_inf = 3.66 (ht docs, original Hausen 1943 not opened); laminar entrance lengths 0.0575 Re D and 0.05 Re Pr D (Wikipedia citing Bergman & Incropera, secondary); Taylor-Aris D(1 + Pe^2/48) (Wikipedia); 20 K/min ramp (arXiv:1811.06119 Methods).
- **Synthetic:** quartz wall thickness and emissivity, element emissivity, gap conduction, all boat and substrate properties (emissivity, NIR absorptivity, mass, cp, contact conductance, area factor), zone blend length, profile offset sigma, S heat-belt edge (1 cm), all sigmas.
- **Not validated:** quartz network, boat and substrate steady and transient temperatures, buoyancy (CFD has none), NIR heating, the Poiseuille mass-flux assumption in a strongly heated tube (CFD agrees at bulk level), the whole model against any measured temperature.

## Deviations and what failed
- `wall_profile_table` was first a string ParamDef; the dashboard test crashed on it (abs of a str), found in `make test`, so it was moved out of the ParamDefs (an extra values key) before the final run; no numerical effect on the scoring.
- The Momeni alignment uses the falling edge only (the S2 rising edge is cut off at 550 C): written into the pre-registration before scoring.
- The post-hoc matched-ramp diagnostic is labelled post-hoc.
- `growth.state` does not apply `furnace.apply` (file not mine): requested in `docs/handoff/requests-n.md`.
- A first test of the zero-flow limit failed because a steep wall edge is smoothed over ~R by radial conduction even at zero flow (O(10 K)); the test now uses a smooth ramp. This was a test error, not a model change.
